# User Study Progress and Technical Blockers

- The user study is currently in progress, with 20 potential participants contacted and three consent forms received so far.

- A significant technical blocker has emerged where recent codebase changes are causing deployment failures; fixes are underway to ensure stability for the upcoming study.

- Given the difficulty in securing participants, the team agreed that a sample size of 15 to 16 participants will be acceptable, assuming full completion from the remaining candidates.

# Future of Sprint Planning in AI-Integrated Teams

- The team concluded that while the cadence of releases may accelerate from weeks to hours, some form of structured planning is required to align human and AI efforts.

- Sprint planning will evolve into a "Sprint Planning 2.0" model, where the fundamental process involves decomposing a large product backlog into actionable items.

- The planning process will persist as long as complex, multi-agent software engineering tasks require coordination, regardless of the tools or naming conventions adopted.

# Human-AI Collaboration and Governance

- The participants theorized a 50/50 division of labor in software engineering within five years, where AI handles the majority of coding while humans focus on high-level tasks.

- Key human responsibilities will shift toward governance, system architecture, integration management, and quality assurance rather than individual implementation.

- Humans will act as the orchestrators of AI agents, providing the necessary oversight to ensure that complex systems—potentially reaching millions of lines of code—remain coherent and functional.

# Platform Architecture and Flexibility

- The research focus is shifting toward developing a flexible, protocol-based marketplace architecture that allows various agents and humans to interact seamlessly.

- The platform will function independently of the agents' internal logic, requiring only that participants adhere to a specific API contract to join the collective workspace.

- By utilizing a "contract net protocol," the system will provide a common interface that enables multiple stakeholders—including different companies—to integrate their agents for collaborative development without needing proprietary technical alignment.

# Research Roadmap and Implementation Strategy

- To prevent scope creep and maintain progress, the team decided to implement a hard-coded version of the platform first to serve as a functional baseline.

- Following the successful deployment of the initial version, the team will generalize the architecture to support greater modularity and agent interoperability.

- The team emphasized the need to avoid over-theorizing future states and instead focus on immediate, incremental implementation to meet project deadlines.

# Academic Writing and Evaluation Metrics

- The next step for the research paper is to draft the introduction, motivation, and context sections to solidify the research focus and specific questions.

- Evaluation metrics for the platform will focus on system openness, scalability, and the effectiveness of the human-AI interaction model established during the study.

- These written materials will be used to clarify the research contribution, framing the platform as a foundational system for the future of professional software engineering collaborations.
